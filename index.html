Core Concept

Meaning and Concept of Break-Even Analysis

Equilibrium Point

The Break-Even Point is the level where Total Revenue = Total Cost.

No Profit, No Loss

At BEP, the business has covered all costs but has not yet earned any profit.

Minimum Sales Target

It helps find the minimum sales volume required to avoid losses.

Margin of Safety

BEP helps management judge how much sales can fall before the firm enters the loss zone.

Cost Classification

Classification of Costs

Cost behaviour explains how expenses react when output changes. Three major cost types are important in break-even analysis.

Type 01

Fixed Cost

Remains constant in total within the relevant range, regardless of production volume.

Examples: Rent, Insurance, Salaries
Type 02

Variable Cost

Changes directly with output. Variable cost per unit normally stays constant.

Examples: Raw Material, Direct Labour, Commission
Type 03

Semi-Variable Cost

Contains both fixed and variable elements and often changes in steps.

Examples: Electricity, Telephone, Maintenance
Key Definitions

Important Terms in Break-Even Analysis

Key Term

Contribution

The amount left after variable cost is deducted from selling price. It contributes first toward fixed cost and then toward profit.

Contribution = Selling Price per Unit − Variable Cost per Unit
Key Term

Profit Volume Ratio

P/V Ratio shows contribution as a percentage of sales. A higher ratio usually means profit rises faster after crossing BEP.

P/V Ratio = (Contribution ÷ Sales) × 100
Core Formulas

Break-Even Formulas

Formula 01

BEP in Units

Fixed Cost ÷ Contribution per Unit
Formula 02

BEP in Rupees

Fixed Cost ÷ P/V Ratio
Formula 03

Margin of Safety

Actual Sales − Break-Even Sales
Important Note: While calculating BEP in rupees, convert the P/V ratio into decimal form. Example: 40% should be written as 0.40.
Worked Example

Numerical Example

Given Data

Fixed Cost (FC)₹ 50,000
Selling Price (SP)₹ 100 / unit
Variable Cost (VC)₹ 60 / unit

Step-by-Step Calculation

Contribution = 100 − 60 = ₹ 40 / unit

BEP in Units = 50,000 ÷ 40 = 1,250 units

BEP in Sales = 1,250 × 100 = ₹ 1,25,000

Risk and Leverage

Relationship with Operating Leverage

Operating leverage explains how strongly profit reacts when sales change. It depends mainly on the firm’s cost structure.

Higher Fixed Costs Raise BEP

When fixed cost increases, a business must achieve higher sales before it reaches the no-profit, no-loss point.

Higher Fixed Costs Increase Operating Leverage

A larger fixed-cost burden makes profit more sensitive to even small changes in sales.

Higher Leverage Means Greater Risk

If sales fall below expectation, losses rise faster because fixed costs still have to be paid.

After BEP, Profit Rises Faster

Once fixed costs are recovered, each additional unit contributes more directly toward profit.

Critical Evaluation

Uses and Limitations of Break-Even Analysis

✓ Uses of BEA

✓
Profit Planning

Helps set realistic sales targets, budgets, and profit goals.

✓
Required Sales Estimation

Shows the volume needed to avoid losses or achieve a target profit.

✓
Pricing Decisions

Supports analysis of how price changes affect contribution and BEP.

× Limitations of BEA

×
Constant Selling Price Assumption

It assumes selling price remains constant and ignores discounts or price fluctuations.

×
Linear Cost and Revenue Assumption

It assumes cost and revenue move in straight lines, which may not happen in reality.

×
Sales Mix Issue

In multi-product firms, a changing product mix can alter the overall break-even point.
